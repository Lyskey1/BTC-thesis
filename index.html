The evidence

Fourteen years, one rhythm.

Every ~4 years the halving cuts new supply in half. The same sequence follows each time: a bull run, a top 17 to 18 months later, a fall that lasts about a year, then quiet accumulation.

Same song, quieter

Each top is a smaller multiple.

Line the four cycles up on their halving day and the shape repeats. What shrinks is the size: from the halving price to the top, each run pays less than the one before. The market gets bigger, the rocket fuel thins out.

Every bear is a little shallower.

From top to bottom, the falls went −94%, −87%, −84%, −77.5%. This bear has reached −54.5% so far. The thesis puts the floor between −56% and −68%, which is $40K–$55K.

The asymmetry: buying three months early or late costs little, because prices flatline near the bottom. Selling three months late is expensive, because the fall after a top runs about a year. So buy over a wide window, and start selling before the top.

How the bottom window and zone are set

Time. The longest mature bear ran 411 days (Nov 29, 2013 → Jan 14, 2015). The window is that length ±5% for the core (day 390–432, Oct 31 → Dec 12, 2026) and ±10% for the outer edge (day 370–452, Oct 11, 2026 → Jan 1, 2027).

Depth. A target of $40K–$55K, which is −56% to −68% from the $126,198 top. Past bears went −94%, −87%, −84% and −77.5%; a shallower floor continues that trend.

Which one wins. A bear ends on time or on depth, whichever comes first. If the window closes without the zone, the time signal still says the worst is likely behind.

Is this financial advice?

No. Four cycles are not a statistic, and every cycle has broken the previous one's script somewhere; the missed October call is the latest example. This page exists for the record, and for the fun of it. Do your own research.
